      How it works

      No server, no API keys, no database: GitHub runs the model and the result is this static page.

      1 · Every minute: forecast

      36 signals are computed from the last four hours of ADA and Bitcoin trading: buy/sell pressure, bid-ask bounce, Bitcoin lead-lag, momentum, range position, volume and time of day. Six experts turn them into forecasts for 5, 15 and 60 minutes ahead, and the council combines those into a probability and a fan of prediction bands.

      2 · Every minute: learn

      When a forecast's time comes it is scored. Three online learners update straight away. Hedge shifts trust toward the experts with the smallest recent errors. Adaptive conformal inference widens a band after misses and narrows it after hits until it holds its promised coverage. Online logistic calibration keeps the stated probabilities honest.

      3 · Every day: evolve

      At 00:00 UTC a GitHub Actions job pulls up to 68 days of 1-minute candles. It runs a champion-vs-challenger tournament over regularisation, training windows, feature sets and tree settings, scored walk-forward on the five most recent days, then retrains every expert on fresh data.

      4 · In public, reproducible

      A few times a day, whenever GitHub's scheduler gets to it, the pipeline replays every minute since its last run with the published model, logs each forecast and its result to CSV files and commits them to git. Your browser runs the same code on the live Binance stream from that checkpoint, so everything on this page, scores included, is complete up to the current minute and matches what gets recorded.

      The honest part

      Short-term crypto prices are close to a random walk. The best academic and competition models are right only slightly more than half the time over a few minutes, and this one is no exception: expect roughly 51–53% at five minutes and close to a coin flip at an hour. Two things it can do reliably are to say how uncertain the next hour is (the bands) and to know when it has no edge. The z-score on the scoreboard uses only non-overlapping forecasts, so a lucky streak can't pass as skill. Part of the short-horizon edge comes from market microstructure (bid-ask bounce) that you couldn't trade profitably after fees. This is a public experiment, not financial advice.
